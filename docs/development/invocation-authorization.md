# Invocation authorization policy boundary

Governed invocation admission, runtime execution, and cancellation share one provider-independent authorization request. A deployment can bind that request to an immutable policy bundle through the opt-in policy-bound decision adapter in `internal/reconcile`.

The policy source resolves by exact isolation domain, service, and revision. A returned bundle must use the closed `dataground.invocation-authorization-policy/v1` contract, repeat that complete scope, provide a portable policy-set identifier, keep both Cedar schema and policy bytes within their independent bounds, and match the length-delimited SHA-256 digest of those bytes. Invalid or scope-drifted bundles fail before evaluation. Policy bytes are copied at the boundary so neither the source nor evaluator can mutate another component's state.

Deployment code constructs a bundle with `NewInvocationAuthorizationPolicy`, which validates the exact scope, portable policy-set identifier, and independent byte bounds before taking ownership of schema and policy content and computing the contract digest. Callers do not supply or duplicate digest logic. Bundle construction does not parse Cedar or select a schema; the concrete evaluator separately requires `CanonicalInvocationCedarSchema` and validates the policy document at decision time.

The repository provides an immutable in-process policy source for explicit deployment configuration. Construction requires at least one valid bundle, rejects duplicate isolation/service/revision scopes even when their bytes match, and takes ownership of every bundle. Resolution is exact and returns an owned copy; missing, malformed, or cancelled lookups fail closed without wildcard, latest-revision, or fallback behavior. The source does not read files or environment variables and does not reload policy implicitly.

Durable mode also provides a PostgreSQL-backed exact-scope source. An authorized operator installs a reviewed Cedar file through `dataground-policy-install`; the command requires the canonical schema, parses the policy before storage, binds it to one existing service revision, and atomically records installer, correlation, policy digest, and reason digest without retaining the free-text reason in audit metadata. The append-only table rejects update and delete operations. Identical installation replay is read-only, while changed bytes, policy identity, installer, correlation, or reason under the same revision scope conflict. Every lookup reconstructs an owned bundle and revalidates its contract, scope, content digest, canonical schema, and Cedar policy before an effect can be authorized.

The repository also provides an opt-in evaluator backed by the pinned official Cedar Go engine. A policy bundle must carry the byte-exact canonical DataGround schema returned by `CanonicalInvocationCedarSchema`; schema drift, policy parse failure, an empty policy document, or any evaluation diagnostic fails closed as policy unavailability. The evaluator constructs principal, action, resource, and context directly from the closed request, supplies no external entity source, and does not cache decisions across revision-bound bundles. Only a diagnostic-free Cedar deny becomes the stable authorization-denied outcome. Engine and policy diagnostics are not exposed; cancellation and deadline outcomes remain distinguishable.

`NewStaticCedarInvocationAuthorizer` is the opt-in deployment composition for immutable in-process bundles. It validates the canonical schema and parses every non-empty policy set before returning, then composes the exact-scope source, concrete evaluator, policy-bound decision, and shared admission/runtime/cancellation authorizer. Invalid configuration therefore fails before worker use. Authorization still resolves and evaluates the exact revision-bound policy for every consequential effect; the composition adds no fallback, wildcard, mutable reload, or decision cache.\n\nDurable deployments can compose `NewAuditedCedarInvocationAuthorizer` with an exact-scope source and append-only recorder. After a policy resolves, every completed allow, deny, or evaluator-unavailable outcome is withheld until PostgreSQL records the actor, complete durable scope, phase, correlation, policy-set identity, and exact digest. Policy lookup failures and cancelled evaluations are not mislabeled as decisions. The record excludes runtime inputs, prompts, schemas, policy bytes, provider data, and diagnostics; an audit write failure becomes policy unavailability before a phase result is released.

This boundary does not implement public or production policy authoring, approval workflow, signing, distribution, dynamic reload, identity resolution, a default policy, audit export or retention, or default-worker composition. Static, durable, and audited Cedar authorizers remain explicit deployment wiring and are not selected by the repository's worker defaults. It also does not authorize OpenShell calls, configure credentials, or certify live execution. Those capabilities remain blocked until their concrete implementations and conformance evidence are added.


## Cedar evaluator input

The evaluator port receives the closed `dataground.invocation-authorization-cedar/v1` input rather than the normalized runtime request. The durable actor is the `DataGround::Actor` principal, the invocation is the `DataGround::Invocation` resource, and the action is one of `admit`, `run`, or `cancel`. Isolation domain, operation, service, revision, and correlation remain explicit context.

Runtime context exists only for the `run` action and exposes locked approval mode, sandbox mode, output-schema presence, artifact count, and the sorted unique closed artifact kinds. It deliberately excludes prompts, schema bodies, artifact identifiers and paths, names, media types, working directories, model values, native provider identities, and policy content. The concrete evaluator consumes only this mapping and does not recover excluded data through another dependency.
